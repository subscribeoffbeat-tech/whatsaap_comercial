The bits we almost forgot

The plan was solid — these are the gaps that turn "works in a demo" into "works for real."

1

Settled: it's for ONE company

This is the big decision that shapes everything, and it's made: the tool serves one business and that business's customers — not lots of separate companies. That's a gift. It makes the whole thing simpler:

�only one of everything

One WhatsApp number/account, one contact database, one team. No "switch between company A and B."

No data walls

We skip all the tenant-isolation plumbing (the hardest part of SaaS). Every query is just "our data."

Simpler login

Just our staff log in (admin / manager / agent). No sign-up flow for outside businesses.

Single VPS is fine

One company's volume fits comfortably on one server. Just add backups + monitoring (chapter 9).

"Their clients" = the customers we message (rows in the contacts table), not separate logins. Good — that's exactly the simple case.

3

The webhook safety net (3 silent killers)

The webhook is where most home-built tools quietly break. Three things to get right:

1 · Save the raw payload first

Before doing anything, write Meta's raw message to webhook_log. If processing crashes, nothing is lost and you can replay it.

2 · Dedupe (idempotency)

Meta re-sends webhooks on its own retries. Same message can arrive twice. Key on wa_message_id so you never double-save or double-auto-reply.

3 · Media expires — grab it fast

When a customer sends a photo/doc, Meta gives a temporary link. Download & store it immediately (to media_path), or the chat history loses its attachments.

+ verify the signature

Check X-Hub-Signature-256 on every call so nobody can fake inbound messages to your endpoint.

4

Consent — getting it IN and OUT

Part 3 said "only message opt-ins." But we never said how people opt in, or how they leave. Both halves are needed:

Opt-IN (collect + record)

Ways a contact becomes opted_in = true: a checkbox at checkout, a website form, replying to a "click-to-WhatsApp" link, or texting a keyword first. Whatever the source, we stamp where & when (opt_in_source, opt_in_at) as proof.

customer texts "STOP"→ we flip opted_in = false→ auto-confirm "you're unsubscribed"→ excluded from all future campaigns

Opt-OUT must be automatic. A keyword like STOP / UNSUBSCRIBE instantly removes them — honoring it by hand is how you collect blocks and reports, which wrecks your quality rating.

5

Connecting the number — the fiddly bits

"Connect WhatsApp" hides a few one-time steps the onboarding screen must handle:

Display name approval

The name customers see (e.g. "Acme Support") has to be approved by Meta and follow naming rules. Can take a little while.

2-step PIN

Registering the number on the Cloud API needs a 6-digit PIN set. Store it safely — you need it to re-register.

Business profile

About text, address, logo, category — fill these so the chat looks legit to customers.

Verify the business

From part 3: do this on day one. Without it you're stuck at 250/day forever.

6

Messages with buttons

Plain text is fine, but the messages that actually convert have buttons. WhatsApp supports a few and our templates & composer should handle them:

Quick-reply buttons

"Yes / No", "Track order / Talk to agent". Customer taps → comes back as a normal inbound message our automation can route on.

Call-to-action buttons

"Visit website" (a link) or "Call us" (a number). These drive the click rates AiSensy brags about.

Why it matters for us: these are stored in the template's buttons field and tracked — a tap is a click we can count and (later) retarget on.

Guard the token

The Meta access token is the master key. Store it encrypted at rest, never in the code or the git repo, loaded from a protected env file.

Audit log

Record who sent which campaign, who changed settings. With several staff sharing one number, you'll want the trail.

9

Keeping it alive

It's one server doing real work for a real business. A little ops discipline saves a lot of pain:

Backups

Nightly pg_dump copied off the VPS (cheap object storage). A database with no backup is a time bomb.

Health + alerts

A /health check, plus alerts when: queue backs up, sends start failing, or quality drops to Yellow.

The runbook

A one-pager for the supervisor: how to restart, where logs are, how to restore a backup, how to rotate the token.

Single-VPS risk

If the box dies, everything stops. Fine for v1 — just keep backups current and know how fast you can spin up a fresh server.

10

The legal bits (worth a real check)

Not legal advice — but these are real, and cheaper to handle now than after a complaint:

India's DPDP Act

You're storing people's personal data (phone, chats). The data-protection law expects consent, a purpose, and a way to delete on request.

WhatsApp Commerce Policy

Meta bans certain goods/claims on WhatsApp. Worth a read before promoting anything sensitive.

Privacy policy + retention

A simple privacy note, and a rule for how long we keep old chats/contacts before purging.

Keep consent proof

The opt_in_source / opt_in_at from chapter 4 is your evidence if anyone asks how you got a number.
