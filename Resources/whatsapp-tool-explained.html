How our WhatsApp tool works

No jargon. Just the idea, the people who use it, and what happens click-by-click — like I'm scribbling it next to you.

1

So… what are we even building?

Imagine a tool that lets a business chat with thousands of customers on WhatsApp — send promos, answer questions, fire off order updates — all from one dashboard. That's apps like AiSensy.

We're building our own version, but living on our own server (VPS) instead of renting someone else's. Why? No monthly software fee, no "pay per agent," and we own all the data.

The one thing we can't build ourselves is WhatsApp itself — so we talk to Meta's official WhatsApp API for the actual sending. Everything around it is ours.

2

Meet the 3 people in the story

Two of them are on our team (the Admin & the Agent). The third is the customer out in the world.

The Admin

the boss / owner. Sets everything up, runs campaigns.

The Agent

support/sales staff. Lives in the chat inbox, talks to people.

The Customer

a regular person on WhatsApp. Never sees our app — just chats.
3

The Admin's journey — from "sign up" to "send"

This is the setup person. They do these steps roughly once, then mostly run campaigns.

1

Signs up & creates the workspace

Enters email + password → a fresh, empty workspace is created just for their business. Right now it's a blank slate.

2

Connects WhatsApp (the Meta part)

Links their verified business + phone number to Meta and pastes in an access key. This is the only "outside" connection — it's how messages physically leave and arrive. Done once.

3

Imports contacts & tags them

Uploads their customer list (people who agreed to be messaged — important!) and sticks labels on them like vip, new-lead, delhi. Tags are how you target campaigns later.

4

Writes message templates → sends to Meta for approval

For promos/notifications you can't just type anything — WhatsApp must pre-approve the wording (so businesses don't spam). The admin drafts them; Meta reviews in a few minutes-to-hours and says ✅ or ❌.

5

Invites the agents

Adds team members by email. Each gets a login so several people can answer the same WhatsApp number at once without stepping on each other.

6

Builds a campaign

Picks who (a tag/segment), what (an approved template), and when (now or scheduled). Hits Send.

7

Watches it go out & checks results

The system paces the sending (more on that later) and the admin sees a live tally: delivered, read, failed. Done!

4

The Agent's journey — a day in the inbox

The agent doesn't set anything up. They just talk to people, all day, from a shared inbox.

1

Gets the invite & logs in

Clicks the invite link, sets a password, lands in the dashboard.

2

Sees the shared inbox

One screen with every live conversation — like a team WhatsApp Web. New messages pop in instantly (no refresh).

3

A chat gets routed to them

When a customer writes in, the system hands the chat to a free agent (or one matching a tag, e.g. all "billing" chats to the billing person).

4

Replies live

Opens the chat, sees the full history, types back. Because the customer messaged first, there's a free 24-hour window to chat normally (no template needed, no cost).

5

Tags, notes, resolves

Labels the customer (interested, refund), jots a private note, then marks the chat done. Next time that person writes, the history's all there.

6

Hands off if stuck

Can pass a tricky chat to a manager. Nothing is lost — it just moves to another agent's queue.

5

What the customer actually feels

From their side it's just… WhatsApp. They never know there's a whole system behind it. Two things can happen:

They message us first

→ maybe an instant auto-reply ("Hi! Type 1 for orders…")
→ or it quietly lands in an agent's inbox and a human replies. That message opened the free 24-hour window.

We message them first

→ a broadcast arrives: a sale, an order update, an OTP. If they reply, it flips into the case on the left — straight to a human.

6

Peeking under the hood (still simple, promise)

There are really just two directions messages flow, and a tiny crew of parts handling them:

⬅️ Messages coming IN

Meta rings our "doorbell" (a webhook). Our app catches it, saves it, checks if a robot can answer — if not, shows it to an agent.
webhook → save → auto-reply or route

➡️ Messages going OUT

A campaign drops thousands of messages into a to-do list (a queue). A worker pulls them one-by-one at a safe speed and hands each to Meta.
queue → paced worker → Meta

Why the "safe speed"? If we blast everything at once, WhatsApp thinks we're spam and can ban the number. So the worker is like a polite person handing out flyers — steady, not shouting. If a send fails, it waits and quietly tries again.

7

4 ideas that explain 90% of the "rules"

🕒 The 24-hour window

When a customer messages you, a 24-hour clock starts. Inside it, you can chat freely & for free. After it closes, you can only reach them with a pre-approved template.

📝 Templates need approval

Any message you start (promos, OTPs, updates) must use wording Meta okayed first. Keeps WhatsApp from turning into spam.

🐢 Slow & steady sending

New numbers start with small daily limits and grow as you behave well. Pacing + good behaviour = higher limits over time.

✅ Only message opt-ins

Message people who said "yes." Buying random lists and blasting them is the #1 way to get the number killed.

The toolbox (in plain words)

Chosen to be cheap to run and simple for one AI dev + one supervisor to manage.

Go
The engine. One strong, fast program that does all the work and handles thousands of sends at once. Ships as a single file — easy to deploy.
PostgreSQL
The filing cabinet. Stores contacts, messages, tags — everything, safely.
River
The to-do list. The queue that holds outbound messages, living inside Postgres (so no extra software).
HTMX
The dashboard. Keeps the screen simple and lets the same Go program draw the UI — fewer moving parts.
Our VPS
Our house. The rented server it all runs on. We control it; no monthly software fees.
Meta API
The post office. The only outside service — it does the actual WhatsApp delivery.
